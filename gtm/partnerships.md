# Partnerships — Borrow Trust and Distribution

Partnerships convert other people's trust into your pipeline. Every play in this section works the same way: give first, make the partner's life easier, and make the value flow measurable so you keep investing in what works.

## Warm intros

Ask for intros through the person who already has the trust — customers and investors, not cold partners.

- How: when a deal closes or a customer succeeds, ask the champion for 1-2 intros to companies like theirs. Make it effortless: draft the forwardable blurb for them.
- Investor intros: quarterly ask, targeted — name the accounts (from the list, `icp-and-tam.md`) and why each fits. Investors make intros for portfolio companies who make it easy.
- Every intro gets the warm treatment: reference the connector by name in the first line, respond same day. A mishandled warm intro burns the connector's credibility — that's the real currency being spent.

## Customer referrals

The best-performing referral programs are simple and fast to redeem.

- Ask at the moment of delight: after a successful rollout, a saved deal, a compliment. Not on an invoice date.
- Reward both sides (customer and referred company) — discounts or credits beat cash for B2B; keep the rules one paragraph long. Programs with a 5-step terms page get ignored.
- Track referrals in the CRM as their own source (feeds `reporting.md` pipeline by motion) so the program earns its keep or gets killed.

## Revenue share

Pay partners a cut when they bring revenue. The design decisions that matter:

- Rate: 10-20% of first-year contract value is the standard band; recurring-for-life or first-year-only is a real strategic choice — decide once, in writing.
- Register the deal: the partner submits the opportunity *before* it advances, so you're not negotiating after the fact. A simple registration form beats a 12-page agreement for the first 20 partners.
- Pay fast, report visibly. Partners who wait 90 days and can't see status stop referring. A shared status doc (or a channel) does more for partner volume than a higher rate.

## Integration listings

Marketplace/app-directory listings are passive pipeline: buyers browse them with budget in hand.

- List on the marketplaces your ICP already shops (depends on your stack adjacency), with a listing written in persona language (`icp-and-tam.md`) — the buyer's job-to-be-done, not your feature list.
- Each integration gets its own landing page answering "what does this do for me" — indexed and answer-first (see the `aio` skill).
- Co-marketing with the integration partner (a joint webinar, a swap of newsletters — see `content.md`) doubles the distribution of one artifact.

## Shared accounts

Two teams selling into the same account should never surprise each other.

- Rule: any account both a partner and you touch gets a shared plan — one doc or record entry naming: who owns which contacts, what each side is doing, current status. Updated at each meaningful change.
- Protect the customer above the deal: if your partner's contact is your competitor's contact, the shared plan decides who talks to whom *before* both of you email the same person in the same week.
- Shared-account deals route through one owner in the CRM (`revops.md`) even when two companies work it — dual-tracked attribution is how the weekly deal review gets lied to.

## Decision Framework

- **Which partner motion first?** Warm intros and customer referrals — they need no new infrastructure and compound with delivery quality. Revenue share and marketplaces come after you have proof and process.
- **Invest more in a partner or a channel?** The one with measured pipeline and a partner who actually responds. One great partner beats ten logos on a slide.
- **Partner wants exclusivity?** Only for a real commitment (quota, co-marketing, dedicated resource), written down with an end date.
