# Inbound and Pipeline — Convert Interest into Meetings, Revive What Died

Inbound leads arrive pre-qualified by their own behavior; pipeline work keeps deals moving and recovers the ones that stalled. The through-line: every contact in this file has already shown intent — the job is to meet that intent with speed and context, not more volume.

## Inbound lead enrichment

Every inbound lead gets enriched before a human looks at it — the same enrichment waterfall as `data-and-enrichment.md`, run on arrival, so the rep sees the whole picture at first touch.

- Enrich: company, size, role, tech stack, and — most important — what they did on your site or in the product before converting.
- Route by fit: ICP match goes to a named owner inside minutes (`revops.md` SLAs); non-ICP gets self-serve and a nurture track, not a rep's morning.
- Do not let leads sit unenriched in a queue. An inbound lead worked without context produces a generic first call, and generic first calls lose warm buyers.

## Meeting prep and follow-up

The rep walks in knowing everything the system knows.

- Automated pre-call brief per meeting: company snapshot, account's signals (which triggers fired — see `signals.md`), what they've seen/engaged with, CRM history, persona notes (`icp-and-tam.md`).
- The brief lands before the call, in the rep's workflow, not in a tool they have to remember to open.
- Follow-up within hours, same day: recap in the buyer's words (persona language), the one next step with a date. Next steps without dates are wishes — they don't count in the weekly deal review (`revops.md`).

## Product sign-up outreach

Free-trial and sign-up users are the highest-intent contacts in the whole system — they're using the product. Work them deliberately.

- Trigger on behavior, not on a timer: sign-up (day 0 welcome that references their signup context), activation milestone reached or missed, power usage, team invite (a team invite is a champion — treat them as one).
- Outreach references what they actually did in-product, framed as help toward their job, not as an audit.
- Non-activated signups get one helpful nudge, then nurture; don't chase the dead ones for months.

## Webinar and event attendees

An event attendee has given you an hour — the follow-up decides what that hour was worth.

- Before: the registration list gets enriched and tiered; tier-1 attendees get personal outreach asking what they want answered.
- During: capture questions and participation. A good question in the chat is persona language, free (`icp-and-tam.md`).
- Within 24h: recording + the specific segment-relevant follow-up per attendee tier. Attendees route like inbound (above) — enriched, routed, owned. The webinar is the signal; the follow-up is the motion.

## Closed-lost revival

Lost deals are a pipeline source you already paid for. Revival is scheduled, not opportunistic.

- Tag every loss with a reason (`revops.md`) and a revival date: "no budget → revisit next quarter," "chose competitor → revisit at contract renewal," "bad timing → revisit next fiscal year."
- The trigger for revival outreach: the date arriving, a new signal on the account (funding, new leader — see `signals.md`), or a product change that answers the original objection.
- Revival first lines reference the history honestly: "when we last talked, X was the blocker — that changed."
- Run the list monthly. Wins from revival are often the cheapest deals on the board, and the loss-reason→revival loop is what makes losing tolerable.

## Decision Framework

- **Speed or polish on inbound touch?** Speed. A good-enough reply in an hour beats a perfect one tomorrow.
- **Revive or delete a dead account?** Delete if the loss reason is permanent (structural no-fit); revive if it was timing, budget, or competitor.
- **Follow-up without a next step and date?** It didn't happen. Write the next step into the deal record.
