# Content — Build Demand You Don't Have to Cold-Start

Content in a GTM system has three jobs: make the founder visible where buyers look, capture the questions buyers actually ask, and give every other motion material to send. Content that serves none of those three is decoration.

## Founder and team posts

Distribution runs through people, not the company page. Pick 1-2 people who can write in their own voice, and protect their consistency.

- Cadence: 2-3 posts per week, per person. Consistency compounds reach; bursts don't.
- What performs: first-person build stories, strong opinions with reasoning, lessons from real deals. What doesn't: press-release voice, engagement-bait, anything the person wouldn't say at a bar.
- Write in persona language (`icp-and-tam.md`). If your buyer wouldn't stop scrolling on it, it's content for your peers, not your pipeline.
- Posts that hit get recycled: top post of the month → newsletter feature → gated asset (see below). One idea, three surfaces.

## Buyer questions from calls

The most valuable content input is free and already happening: the questions buyers ask on calls.

- Harvest: reps log (or transcripts surface) every question asked twice — objections, comparison questions, "how does X work with Y," pricing questions.
- Each recurring question becomes an artifact, in order of how often it stalls deals: a one-paragraph answer reps can paste, then a full page/post that answers it in the first sentence (question-style headings, answer-first — see the `aio` skill for the extraction layer).
- This loop keeps content tied to deals instead of to the editorial calendar's mood. Review the question backlog monthly in the deal review (`revops.md`).

## Weekly newsletter

One send, weekly, to everyone who gave you an email address. The newsletter is the retention layer for every other motion.

- Format: one main idea (often the week's best post, rewritten shorter) + a short list of things worth their attention. Skimmable in 90 seconds.
- The list is worked like any other pipeline: clicks and replies are signals routed per `signals.md`.
- Never skip two weeks in a row. A dead newsletter is worse than no newsletter — it teaches subscribers to ignore the sender address.

## Live sessions and clips

Long-form material is mined, not filmed and forgotten.

- One monthly live session (demo, teardown, Q&A with a customer) generates: the recording, 4-8 short clips, a recap post, and 5-10 quoted lines for future copy.
- Clips get distributed natively (no link-drops) and reused in outbound — a 40-second clip answering a persona's exact question is a better email attachment than any PDF.
- The session topics come from the buyer-questions backlog (above): answer what deals are stalling on.

## Gated templates

Templates, checklists, calculators — artifacts buyers would pay for, gated to capture intent.

- Gate only what's worth an email: a real template your team actually uses, not a PDF of your deck. A weak gate poisons trust in every future gate.
- Gated downloads are hot signals: route to owner (`revops.md`), follow up referencing the artifact, not "I saw you downloaded."
- Every gated asset links to the ungated version of its core idea on the site — the gate captures, the page ranks and gets cited (see the `aio` skill).

## Decision Framework

- **Write for buyers or peers?** Buyers. Peer content earns applause, buyer content earns meetings. Check: would a persona forward this to a teammate with a "look at this"?
- **New idea or deeper on an existing one?** Deeper. One idea across five formats beats five ideas across one.
- **Content not moving deals?** Check the buyer-questions loop — if the backlog hasn't been harvested this quarter, content is running on imagination.
- **Company page or people?** People, every time. Boost the best ones as promoted posts (`abm-and-paid-ads.md`) if paid is in play.
