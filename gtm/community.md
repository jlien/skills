# Community — Be Present Where the Buyers Already Gather

Community motions are slow to start and disproportionately durable: the trust built in a room (physical or digital) converts better than any sequence. The rule that keeps it honest: show up as a participant, not as a vendor with a lanyard.

## Podcasts

Two directions: guest on the podcasts your buyers listen to, and only then consider hosting your own.

- Guesting: pitch specific episodes — a sharp, contrarian take on a problem your personas own (`icp-and-tam.md`), not a company story. One good episode earns authority and a clip library (reuse per `content.md`).
- Hosting: only after 10+ guest appearances teach you what conversations hold. A podcast that dies at episode 4 costs more credibility than it ever earned.
- Every appearance: clip it, quote it in outbound where relevant, put the best 40 seconds where retargeting can amplify it (`abm-and-paid-ads.md`).

## Dinners

Small, senior, curated. 6-10 people, one table, one topic.

- Curate the guest list: mix of target-account execs (tier 1 list), a customer or two, and a peer to keep conversation honest. Never seat more than one person per company.
- Topic over pitch: one question the whole table can argue about. The host (founder) talks least.
- Follow-up within 48h: photos, one takeaway, individual notes referencing what each person said. The dinner is the signal; the follow-up is the motion (same pattern as `inbound-and-pipeline.md`).
- Cost per dinner is high — measure it against meetings-from-dinners in `reporting.md` or run it as brand spend knowingly.

## Launches

Product launches through community are earned before the launch date.

- Seed with the people who asked for the feature (the buyer-questions and product-signup loops — `content.md`, `inbound-and-pipeline.md`): early access, a named thank-you, their feedback quoted in the launch.
- Launch to communities where you already participated. Dropping a launch link into a community that's never seen you is spam with extra steps.
- The launch day artifact is demo-first: 90 seconds showing the thing working, then the writing.

## Paid community

Sponsor or join a paid community (Slack/Discord groups, industry collectives) only where your buyers actually talk shop.

- Before sponsoring: lurk for a month. If the conversations don't touch your personas' problems, the logo on the sponsor page buys nothing.
- Give before asking: answer questions, share templates (the gated assets from `content.md`, ungated inside the community), do a free teardown. The first three things you do in a community should cost you something.
- Sponsorships work when they buy you a platform (an AMA, a webinar slot), not just a logo. Ask for the platform.

## Slack and Discord groups

The free version of the above, and the top of the funnel for it.

- Join the 3-5 groups where personas talk. Set a weekly 30-minute participation block — presence decays invisibly, and a ghost member is worse than an absent one.
- Answer questions with real answers; share what works without the pitch. The profile link does the selling.
- Community threads where your product or category comes up are brand-mention signals (`signals.md`) — answer helpfully, disclose affiliation, never astroturf.
- Capture the language: community threads are the purest source of persona phrasing (`icp-and-tam.md`). Quote threads (with permission) in copy.

## Decision Framework

- **Sponsor or participate?** Participate first; sponsor only a community you'd want to belong to anyway.
- **Is a community motion working?** Countable outcomes within a quarter: intros accepted, threads that produced pipeline, dinner-to-meeting rate. Vibes are not a metric — but pick a generous one (relationships compound later).
- **Time budget:** community motions run on founder/leadership time, which is the scarcest resource in the company. One community done well beats five attended rarely.
