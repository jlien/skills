# Skyscraper Approach

A competitive backlink acquisition strategy: find the content competitors rank for, build a better version, and convince everyone who linked to the original to link to yours instead.

## Core Responsibilities

- Identify high-value backlink opportunities from competitor content
- Systematically audit competitor articles for gaps and weaknesses
- Produce superior, more comprehensive content that earns links
- Execute outreach to linkers with specific, credible pitches

## Workflows

### Phase 1: Opportunity Identification

1. Pick a competitor or content category in your niche
2. Find what they rank for on Page 1 of Google (use Ahrefs, Semrush, or manual search)
3. Prioritize by:
   - **Link count**: More backlinks to the original = more outreach targets
   - **Your authority**: Can you credibly outperform this specific piece?
   - **Relevance**: Does this align with your content strategy and audience?

### Phase 2: Gap Analysis

Read the competitor's article and flag weaknesses:

- **Outdated information**: Stats, prices, features, or events that are stale
- **Shallow answers**: Topics mentioned but not explained
- **Missing examples**: No real-world use cases, screenshots, or walkthroughs
- **No data**: Claims without supporting numbers, charts, or research
- **Poor structure**: Hard to scan, missing TOC, no visual breaks
- **Missing formats**: No video, calculator, template, or downloadable asset

Document the gaps — this becomes both your content brief and your outreach ammunition.

### Phase 3: Content Creation

Build a genuinely better resource:

- Cover every point the original does, plus your gaps
- Add original data, examples, or research the original lacks
- Better visual design, structure, and readability
- Include formats they missed (tables, screenshots, tools, templates)
- Update all stats and references to current dates

**Quality bar**: A reasonable person visiting your page should think "this is the best resource on this topic."

### Phase 4: Linker Discovery

Find every website, blogger, and editor who linked to the original content:

- Use backlink tools (Ahrefs, Semrush, Majestic) for the original URL
- Look for:
  - Blog posts and articles linking to it
  - Resource pages and roundups
  - Curated lists and "best of" collections
  - Forum answers (Reddit, Stack Overflow) — these don't pass much link juice but can drive traffic

Build a target list with: contact name, email/LinkedIn, the page that linked, and context about how they used the original.

### Phase 5: Outreach

Reach out via email or LinkedIn with a specific pitch:

1. Reference their existing link to the original
2. Explain what's improved in your version (specific gaps filled)
3. Offer a clear reason to update their link
4. Keep it short and low-pressure

**Pitch framework**:
> "Hey [name] — I saw your [page/post] linking to [original]. It's a solid resource, but I noticed it's a bit outdated on [specific gap]. We just published a more current version with [specific improvements]. If you're open to it, would you consider swapping the link or adding ours as a second resource?"

### Phase 6: Follow-up

- Follow up once if no response (3-5 days)
- Track results: outreach sent, responses, links earned
- Log what worked and what didn't for future iterations

## Decision Framework

**Do this when:**
- A competitor has a high-traffic piece with multiple backlinks
- You have the expertise and resources to genuinely improve on it
- The topic aligns with your content strategy

**Skip this when:**
- The original has very few backlinks (<5) — not worth the outreach effort
- You can't meaningfully improve on the content
- The topic is outside your expertise or brand voice

## Collaboration Patterns

- **Content team**: Drafts and publishes the improved article
- **SEO team**: Identifies opportunities, runs backlink analysis, tracks results
- **Outreach team**: Executes the linker outreach (can be the same person as SEO)
- **Analytics team**: Monitors traffic impact from earned links

## Key Principles

- **People always want to link to the best resource on a given topic** — that's why this works
- The quality of your content determines the quality of your outreach — don't pitch weak content
- Personalization beats volume in outreach — 10 targeted pitches beat 100 generic ones
- Track everything so you can refine your approach over time
- This is not a one-off tactic — it's a repeatable system for building authority
